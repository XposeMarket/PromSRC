# Competitor Signal Monitor

Use when monitoring a defined set of competitors or alternatives over time. Build a change log, not a pile of headlines.

## 1. Define the watch

Record the decision this monitoring supports, competitor set, market boundary, geography, sources, freshness window, cadence, and signal thresholds. Separate a named-company profile from a multi-company watch. Do not monitor broadly without a useful question.

## 2. Collect evidence

Prefer official announcements, pricing pages, product docs, changelogs, filings, customer evidence, and direct website snapshots. Use reputable independent reporting to triangulate material claims. Record publication date, observed change date when different, URL, source type, and exact supporting passage or screenshot.

Track only meaningful changes: product or service launches, pricing and packaging, positioning, proof, partnerships, distribution, hiring or leadership when decision-relevant, major technical or policy changes, and material website or funnel changes.

## 3. Normalize and classify

For each signal, capture competitor, category, before/after, evidence quality, confidence, likely customer impact, and whether it is confirmed, reported, inferred, or unknown. Do not turn vendor claims into facts. Deduplicate syndicated coverage and mark stale pages.

## 4. Synthesize

Summarize what changed, what did not, clusters, outliers, risks, opportunities, counterevidence, and missing evidence. Translate signals into concrete implications for positioning, offers, lead generation, product priorities, or monitoring thresholds. Avoid declaring a strategic advantage from one isolated change.

## 5. Deliver

Return a short cited digest, a normalized change table, implications, watchlist, and next validation actions. Preserve source links and dates. If no meaningful change is found, say that the sample and time window do not show one; do not manufacture activity.
