# Weekly Reset Planning

Use at the start or end of a week when the goal is to reduce open loops and choose a realistic focus. Prefer a short executable plan over an exhaustive life audit.

## 1. Gather the current state

Declare the source of truth for tasks, calendar, projects, and user-stated commitments up front. Collect active projects, commitments, calendar constraints, unfinished tasks, deadlines, blockers, recent wins, financial or revenue obligations, and anything consuming attention. Use connected sources only when available and authorized. Check `tool_search` for a calendar connection; if absent, say so and plan from tasks plus user-stated commitments without inventing events. Mark stale or missing information.

## 2. Close loops

For each open item, recommend: do, schedule, delegate, defer with a reason, or delete. Extract the next physical action, owner, due date, dependency, and definition of done. Surface commitments that have no owner or deadline instead of silently carrying them forward. Make a dedicated "waiting on others" pass: identify who owes what, when to follow up, and what can move meanwhile. Silence is not evidence of completion.

## 3. Choose focus

Select one to three weekly outcomes based on impact, urgency, available time, energy, and dependencies. Protect deep-work blocks and include maintenance, admin, recovery, and contingency time. Do not create a plan that assumes every hour is available.

## 4. Make the plan

Return: wins, at-risk items, waiting on others, stalled items, one to three next-week outcomes, proposed updates, coverage gaps, and a daily or milestone sequence. Keep actions concrete and small enough to start. Include a midweek check and a short end-of-week review question. Recommend rather than mutate calendar or tasks by default; make changes only when explicitly directed and authorized, then read back approved writes. Offer to schedule the review weekly with `schedule_job` if useful, but never schedule it without direction.

## 5. Verify reality

Compare the plan against the actual calendar and known capacity. State assumptions, unresolved decisions, and what should trigger replanning. If a financial or trading item is included, keep it as tracking and process support, not a promise of returns or a substitute for judgment.

## Completion gate

Before reporting the reset complete, verify each selected next action has an owner, a concrete first step, and a review point; label missing inputs instead of inventing commitments.
